# Roku vs Samsung catalog: title-by-title comparison (2026-11-23)

Read-only comparison of the production catalogs (Roku 40 titles, Samsung 12; every Samsung title is also on Roku).
Being on Roku is not treated as proof of clearance.

## Why the counts differ
Both apps are PROtv-owned and both store listings are **United States only**. The difference is a backend rule.
- **Roku** accepts U.S. evidence: public-domain findings for the U.S., U.S.-only grants, and the
  October Roku/Fire TV reviewed list. It relies on the U.S.-only store listing; there is no server-side country check.
- **Samsung** (decision of 2026-11-14) accepts only evidence that is **worldwide** and covers PROtv's apps generally,
  "so the Samsung app adds no new geographic exposure", until server-side territory controls exist.
So most of the gap (19 titles) is the worldwide-only rule, not a contract.

## Issues that also affect Roku today
- **Unresolved film copyright:** 6 titles (tier C) are on Roku with only Internet Archive labels as evidence.
- **Music and source copy:** for all 16 credible public-domain titles, the soundtrack has not been reviewed
  separately and the source of PROtv's copy is not recorded. This applies equally to Roku.
- **Elephants Dream:** the CC BY version question is open, and the title is on Roku.
- **Dead Air:** the agreement says the signer's authority must be confirmed before issuance; it is still unconfirmed,
  and the title is on Roku.
- **The Thirst:** the contract says PROtv "will use its available territorial-access controls" to limit playback to
  the U.S. Roku currently relies only on the U.S.-only store listing.
- **CC BY films on both platforms** (Big Buck Bunny, Sintel, Tears of Steel): the source copy is unconfirmed against
  the official releases.
